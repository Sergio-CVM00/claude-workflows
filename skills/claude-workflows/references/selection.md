# Select the smallest sufficient deployment

Apply the catalog to the inspected task. The quality and acceptance floor comes first;
among options that meet it, prefer less latency and coordination. These are decision checks,
not a ladder that every task must climb.

Before recommending delegation, identify:
- The capability gap: independent evidence, isolated context, specialized investigation,
  useful alternative proposals, or enough repeatable inventory to justify orchestration.
- The partition: self-contained briefs, dependencies, stable IDs and exclusive ownership.
- The integration plan: how findings or patches combine, who resolves contradictions,
  expected coverage, and the global checks that establish the deliverable.
- The overhead: duplicated context, scheduling, review and integration. State uncertainty;
  avoid invented token estimates or thresholds copied from other engines.

If no concrete gap remains, recommend the main session. A second agent must have a useful
assignment; expertise labels alone do not justify one. Prefer a few ordinary subagents when
the main session can coordinate them. Choose a saved native workflow for a coordination or
reuse need, not merely because several subtasks exist.

Known inventory supports a fixed partition. Dynamic discovery is appropriate when the
inventory genuinely cannot be specified beforehand. Routing among known task categories
belongs to selection: use explicit rules where possible, reject unknown categories and
ambiguous assignments, and do not introduce an extra model call just to classify obvious work.

Before fan-out, specify integration and acceptance. Rework the partition if merging is likely
to dominate execution. A tightly coupled shared registry can require a main-session pilot or
sequential integration even when hundreds of files are involved.

For proposals, write the common rubric before drafting. Give candidates independent briefs
and the same acceptance criteria. Judge every candidate against every criterion before
comparison; retain evidence and reasons for rejected candidates. Select keeps one intact;
fusion creates a new artifact that needs fresh acceptance. One structured judging call is
the bundled default; its scorecards do not prove the model evaluated independently or fairly.

Offer only materially distinct options. Include a viable main-only route and one recommended
choice. A choice authorizes the stated envelope, not automatic escalation. On a new blocker,
retain evidence and recommend the smallest change that addresses the demonstrated gap.

These are owned design rules informed by [the prior-art comparison](prior-art.md), with
quality first and speed second as the explicit user policy.
